1 · The business problem

NOVA MART's category team faced the classic mid-size retailer's dilemma: discounting visibly moves volume, but nobody could say which discounts paid for themselves. Four questions kept recurring — which products to discount and by how much, which segments to target, when inventory (not marketing) should drive the decision, and when the right answer is to not discount at all.

PRISM is a decision-support product, not a dashboard. Its unit of output is a priced action: "run a 12% targeted promotion on this SKU for this segment; expect this much incremental contribution margin, at this confidence, for these reasons."

DATA→ ANALYSIS→ PREDICTION→ OPTIMIZATION→ DECISION

2 · The data (synthetic, but statistically honest)

Because no real retailer hands its price book to a portfolio project, I generated a synthetic company — but deliberately not a random one. The generator builds causality first, numbers second: demand follows a constant-elasticity response Q = A·P−E with category-level elasticities (Accessories ≈ 2.0 down to Cameras ≈ 0.8); customer archetypes modulate discount response (Deal Seekers buy on promo, Premium Loyalists largely don't); inventory depletes with real (s,S) replenishment and stockouts that cap sales; and the festive calendar (Oct–Nov peak, Republic Day, Independence Day) drives seasonality the way Indian retail actually experiences it.

This matters for one reason: the analytics have to earn their answers. Nothing downstream reads the "true" parameters — every elasticity, uplift and forecast in this report was estimated from the transaction data alone.

3 · What the analytics found

3.1 Price sensitivity is measurable — and unevenly distributed

Elasticities are estimated per SKU with within-product log-log regressions on price-level aggregates (promo depth buckets), seasonally adjusted using the market demand index. The estimates recover the designed causal structure with expected attenuation (~20–40%, count noise at SKU-week level is real), and — more importantly — preserve the ranking that pricing decisions need.

3.2 Promotions work — which is exactly the problem

Analysing 122 promotion events with pre/during/post baselines (seasonally adjusted, cannibalisation measured on non-scoped same-category SKUs):

  • Median sales uplift is +24% — promotions genuinely move demand.
  • Median ROI on campaign cost is -2.1x; 98% of events destroy contribution margin.
  • Flash Sales earn the best uplift; Loyalty Offers are the worst value per rupee spent.
  • Post-promo dips and same-category cannibalisation quietly claw back part of every "win".
Insight that drives the product. Higher sales was being read as success. Once incrementality is netted against discount depth, campaign cost, cannibalisation and post-promo dips, the portfolio conclusion flips: NOVA MART doesn't have a promotion problem, it has a targeting problem. The fix is per-SKU, per-segment decisions — which is what the optimizer does.

3.3 Segments respond to price very differently

Behavioural segmentation (RFM + KMeans k=5 with rule overlays for New / At-Risk states, on 10,000 customers) recovers the designed archetypes — 9908% match on Premium Loyalists, 7746% on Deal Seekers — and the commercial asymmetry is stark:

  • Premium Loyalists — 2,773 customers, 63% of revenue, average discount availed 5%. They buy regardless of price.
  • Deal Seekers — 2,459 customers, 14% of revenue, average discount availed 11%. They are the promotion audience.

Deep discounts aimed at loyal high-spenders are almost pure margin leakage — roughly the single largest avoidable cost the analysis surfaced.

3.4 Inventory changes the answer

Overstocked SKUs should sometimes be discounted even at mediocre elasticity; thin-cover SKUs should never be discounted regardless of elasticity. The optimizer treats inventory days, stockout probability and sell-through as first-class constraints, not reporting afterthoughts.

5 · The decision engine

An optimizer (objective: maximise incremental contribution margin; constraints: ≥8% margin floor, ≤30% discount, price ≥ 85% of competitor, inventory caps, campaign budget) feeds a transparent six-rule cascade. No black box — every recommendation ships with its reasons, expected impact, confidence and the KPIs that drove it. Three real outputs, verbatim:

Note the deliberate balance: "don't discount" recommendations are treated as first-class decisions, not non-events. Roughly 43% of the catalogue earns a hold-price verdict — that discipline is where most of the margin comes from.

6 · Business impact (modelled)

  • ₹31.5 L / quarter of campaign spend currently deployed on loss-making events — the engine flags these pre-launch and redirects budget to margin-accretive targets.
  • ₹0.6 L / quarter of direct incremental contribution across the top-20 targeted actions — small on purpose: value comes from stopping bad promos more than launching new ones.
  • Discount discipline: ₹15.6 Cr of discount cost over 24 months — most of it on SKUs whose demand barely responded. Elasticity screening alone would have caught a large share.
  • Working capital release: ₹8.4 Cr of overstock value has margin-funded exit paths (targeted promotions on elastic, high-margin SKUs only).
  • Segment reallocation: redirecting deep discounts from Premium Loyalists to Deal Seekers preserves the uplift while protecting the margin that funds it.

7 · Limitations & what I'd do next

  • Synthetic data makes estimation cleaner than real-world mess — recovered elasticities are attenuated 20–40% by count noise (visible in Fig 3), and I kept that honestly in the report rather than tuning it away.
  • Uplift is estimated from pre/post baselines, not causal designs. Next iteration: synthetic geo-holdouts so the promo model learns from true experiments.
  • Constant-elasticity response is only valid inside the observed price range; the engine floors extrapolation at ±30%.
  • Confidence scores are heuristic (significance + model quality + data depth). Calibrating them against realized outcomes is on the roadmap, as is a small FastAPI service so the optimizer can be called from the retailer's existing tools.

Appendix · Running the project

  1. Install Python 3.10+, then: pip install -r requirements.txt
  2. Build everything: python3 pipeline.py  (generates data → analytics → models → optimizer cache, ~30 s)
  3. Launch: streamlit run app.py → open http://localhost:8501
  4. Optional: regenerate a fresh dataset python3 pipeline.py --force; headless page tests python3 tests/smoke_test.py
